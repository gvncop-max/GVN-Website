Hands-Free Property Investment: Managed vs DIY in Staffordshire

By Valentine Grey8 August 2026Updated 15 November 20266 min read

Fifty-two percent. That is the share of landlords in the government’s English Private Landlord Survey 2024 who said they use no agent at all, among those who protect tenants’ deposits themselves. It was 52% in 2018 and 49% in 2021. So the idea that investors are rushing to managed lettings isn’t in the official data. What has changed is the work.

The short answer: hands-free property investment means you own a rental property while professionals find it, let it and manage it for a fee. It suits investors who value their time and want the Renters’ Rights Act paperwork done properly. It costs part of the rent, and the legal duties of a landlord still apply to you.

Key points

  • Hands-free property investment means professionals source, let and manage a rental property while the investor owns it.
  • In the English Private Landlord Survey 2024, 52% of landlords who registered deposits themselves used no agent, 43% used letting services and 18% used management services.
  • Since 1 May 2026, the Renters’ Rights Act 2025 has ended section 21 evictions in England; possession needs a section 8 ground.
  • Rent can now be raised only once a year, using Form 4A at least 2 months ahead, and a tenant can ask the First-tier Tribunal to decide the rent.
  • In Stoke-on-Trent, the Office for National Statistics average terraced rent was £726 a month in September 2026, and the HM Land Registry average terraced price was £127,868 in August 2026.
  • Letting agents must belong to a government-approved redress scheme, and property sourcing businesses must register with HM Revenue and Customs for anti-money laundering supervision.

What is hands-free property investment?

Hands-free property investment is an arrangement in which you own a rental property and pay professionals to do the work: finding the property, arranging any refurbishment, finding tenants and running the tenancy. Two services usually sit inside it. Property sourcing is the paid service of finding an investment property for a buyer; HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC), in its anti-money laundering guidance for estate agency businesses (opens in a new tab), treats a business that “introduces buyers or investors to a ‘property deal’” as estate agency work. Full management is the service in which a letting agent finds tenants and handles the tenancy day to day for a fee.

You still own the asset and carry the risk that rents or values fall. What you buy is time.

Are more UK landlords really choosing managed lettings?

The government’s own survey doesn’t show a shift. The English Private Landlord Survey 2024 (opens in a new tab), published on GOV.UK on 5 December 2024, found that 52% of landlords who registered a deposit directly used no agent, “a similar proportion to 2021 (49%) and 2018 (52%)”. Just over two in five (43%) used agent letting services and just under one in five (18%) used agent management services.

The report warns that, because it covers only landlords who registered deposits themselves, agent use may be under-counted. Even so, the rules have changed faster than the habits.

How has the Renters’ Rights Act changed self-managing in 2026?

The Renters’ Rights Act 2025 added deadlines and formal steps to self-managing a let in England from 1 May 2026. The main ones, from GOV.UK:

  • No section 21. GOV.UK’s overview for landlords (opens in a new tab) says you need a legal reason to evict and must serve a section 8 notice using one or more grounds for possession.
  • The Information Sheet. Landlords had to give existing tenants the Renters’ Rights Act Information Sheet 2026 (opens in a new tab) by 31 May 2026, or risk a fine of up to £7,000.
  • Rent increases. GOV.UK’s rent increase guidance (opens in a new tab) says you can raise the rent only once a year, using Form 4A at least 2 months before it starts, and the tenant can ask the First-tier Tribunal to decide the new rent.

More is scheduled. The government’s implementation roadmap (opens in a new tab) puts the landlord database “from late 2026” and expects ombudsman membership for landlords in 2028. Our post on Renters’ Rights Act Phase 2 in the West Midlands covers the database, and our Renters’ Rights Act guide covers what is already in force.

Managed or DIY: how do they compare?

Managed and DIY letting differ in who does the work and who pays for it, not in who the law calls the landlord. GOV.UK’s list of landlord responsibilities (opens in a new tab) is addressed to the landlord: keep the property safe and free from health hazards, keep gas and electrical equipment safe, provide an Energy Performance Certificate, protect the deposit in a government-approved scheme and check the tenant’s right to rent.

TaskSelf-managed (DIY)Hands-free (managed)
Finding the propertyYou search, view and negotiateA sourcing agent finds and checks it for a fee
Finding tenantsYou advertise, view and referenceThe agent does it
Information Sheet, notices, Form 4AYou track the deadlinesThe agent serves them, if the contract says so
Gas, electrical and deposit dutiesYou arrange themThe agent arranges them; they remain landlord duties
Repairs and tenant callsYou answer themThe agent handles them
CostYour timeA fee, set out in the agent’s contract

The contract matters most: get in writing which of these the agent does.

Does a managed service still pay in Stoke-on-Trent?

A managed service can still pay in Stoke-on-Trent if the rent covers the fee with room to spare, and you can test that before you commit. Gross yield is the year’s rent divided by the price, before any costs. The Office for National Statistics (ONS) private rent figures (opens in a new tab) published on 21 October 2026 put the average terraced rent in Stoke-on-Trent at £726 a month in September 2026, or £8,712 a year. HM Land Registry’s UK House Price Index (opens in a new tab) puts the average terraced price in the city at £127,868 in August 2026.

That is a gross yield of about 6.8%. It is an illustration from two official averages that cover different sets of homes, not a figure for any real property. Then the costs come off: each percentage point of rent paid as a management fee is about £87 a year on that rent, and interest, repairs, insurance, empty months and tax come off too. Tax on rental profit rises from April 2027, as our Budget 2026 landlord tax post explains.

Values move both ways. The same index shows the average price of all homes in Stoke-on-Trent down 0.2% in the year to August 2026, and terraced homes up 0.2%. No return is certain.

What should you check before paying for a hands-free service?

Before paying for a hands-free service, check the firm meets the legal basics for its work.

  1. A redress scheme. Under article 3 of the Redress Schemes for Lettings Agency Work and Property Management Work Order 2014 (opens in a new tab), a person doing lettings agency work “must be a member of a redress scheme”, and the Estate Agents (Redress Scheme) Order 2008 (opens in a new tab) does the same for estate agency work. Ask which scheme.
  2. Client money protection. Under the Client Money Protection Schemes for Property Agents Regulations 2019 (opens in a new tab), an agent who holds client money, such as your rent, must belong to an approved or designated scheme.
  3. HMRC registration. HMRC’s guidance says a business “must not trade as a EAB until they have applied to register with HMRC”, and that includes property sourcing.
  4. Fees in pounds. Ask for each fee as a sum, not only a percentage.

Questions investors are asking

What is hands-free property investment?

Hands-free property investment is owning a rental property while professionals do the work: a sourcing agent finds the property, and a letting agent finds tenants and manages the tenancy. You keep the income after fees and carry the risk that rents or values fall. GOV.UK still lists the safety, deposit and right to rent duties as landlord responsibilities.

Are more UK landlords using letting agents?

The government’s English Private Landlord Survey 2024 does not show a shift. It found 52% of landlords who registered deposits directly used no agent, similar to 49% in 2021 and 52% in 2018. In 2024, 43% used agent letting services and 18% used agent management services. The report notes agent use may be under-counted.

Is a managed rental worth the fee in Stoke-on-Trent?

It depends on the rent, the price and your time. Official averages give a Stoke-on-Trent terraced house a gross yield of about 6.8%, from an ONS rent of £726 a month in September 2026 and a Land Registry price of £127,868 in August 2026. Each percentage point of rent paid in fees costs about £87 a year on that rent.

How do I check a property sourcing company is legitimate?

Ask which redress scheme it belongs to, because the Estate Agents (Redress Scheme) Order 2008 requires one for estate agency work. Ask for its HMRC anti-money laundering registration, because HMRC treats property sourcing as estate agency work. If it will hold your money, ask which client money protection scheme it uses, and get every fee in writing as a sum.
