Mortgage Approvals Up, Buy-to-Let Purchases Down: June 2026 Data

By Valentine Grey30 August 2026Updated 15 November 20266 min read

Mortgage approvals rose in June 2026, borrowing jumped and property transactions held up. Yet landlords were refinancing far more than they were buying. That gap is the most useful thing in the summer’s lending data for anyone investing in Stoke-on-Trent or Staffordshire.

The short answer: Bank of England figures show approvals for house purchase rose to 58,200 in June 2026, still below the six-month average of about 61,400, while UK Finance counted more than two buy-to-let remortgages for every buy-to-let purchase in April to June 2026. The market was recovering slowly, and landlords were mostly refinancing what they already owned.

Key points

  • Net mortgage approvals for house purchase rose to 58,200 in June 2026, from 56,600 in May, below the six-month average of around 61,400 (Bank of England).
  • Net mortgage borrowing rose to £7.7 billion in June 2026, from £3.3 billion in May (Bank of England).
  • The effective interest rate on newly drawn mortgages rose to 4.35% in June 2026, from 4.22% in May, and Bank Rate was held at 3.75% on 30 July 2026.
  • UK Finance figures show 16,026 buy-to-let purchase loans in April to June 2026, 845 fewer than in January to March, against 39,631 buy-to-let remortgages.
  • HMRC’s provisional count of UK residential transactions was 98,700 in June 2026 (seasonally adjusted), 2% higher than June 2025.
  • The average house price in Stoke-on-Trent was £151,355 in May 2026, up 5.0% in a year, against £271,295 and 2.7% for the UK (HM Land Registry first estimate).

What did the Bank of England’s June 2026 mortgage data show?

The Bank of England’s June 2026 mortgage data showed approvals and borrowing both rising, but approvals still below their recent average. The Money and Credit release for June 2026 (opens in a new tab), published on 29 July 2026, puts approvals for house purchase at 58,200, up from 56,600 in May.

Three terms are worth pinning down. A mortgage approval is a lender’s agreement to lend for a house purchase, counted before the loan completes; the Bank counts approvals net of cancellations and calls them “an indicator of future borrowing”. Gross lending is the total amount lent secured on homes in the month. Net mortgage borrowing is what is left after repayments.

The remortgage figure only counts switches to a different lender, so a new deal with the same bank doesn’t appear in it. The effective rate is “the actual interest paid”.

Behind that sat Bank Rate. The Bank of England’s Monetary Policy Committee voted by six to three to hold Bank Rate at 3.75% in the decision published on 30 July 2026 (opens in a new tab), with three members voting for a rise to 4%. We’d read June as a market inching back, not surging: buyers returning carefully, and borrowing costs steady rather than falling.

Why were landlords remortgaging instead of buying?

Landlords were mainly refinancing in spring 2026: UK Finance’s buy-to-let figures for April to June 2026 (opens in a new tab), published in October, show 16,026 loans for house purchase, 845 fewer than in the first quarter, against 39,631 remortgages. That is more than two remortgages for every purchase.

The loans being written looked healthy. The average interest cover ratio on new buy-to-let loans was 217% in that quarter, up from 210% a year earlier, and the average rate was 4.82%. The interest cover ratio is the rent as a percentage of the mortgage interest; lenders use it to test whether a property can carry its loan, so 217% means the rent was more than twice the interest.

Our reading: many landlords were sorting out borrowing on homes they already owned before buying more. That isn’t a retreat. It’s the move our guide to buy, refurbish, refinance, repeat describes: release capital from a property that has done its job, then decide what to buy next.

The rules had changed too. The main tenancy reforms of the Renters’ Rights Act 2025, including the end of section 21 “no-fault” evictions, came into force in England on 1 May 2026, as GOV.UK announced (opens in a new tab).

Were UK property transactions rising in June 2026?

UK property transactions held steady in June 2026: HMRC’s provisional count, published on 31 July 2026 (opens in a new tab), was 98,700 seasonally adjusted residential transactions, marginally up from 98,460 in May and 2% higher than June 2025.

That 2% is not flattered by a weak month a year earlier. HMRC says April and May 2025 were depressed because purchases were brought forward into March 2025, ahead of the Stamp Duty Land Tax threshold changes in England and Northern Ireland, but June 2025 had returned to typical levels. It reads the June 2026 rise as a likely increase in underlying activity. Its later tables revise June 2026 to 98,210, still about 3% above June 2025.

What does the June 2026 lending data mean for investors in Stoke-on-Trent?

For Stoke-on-Trent, the June 2026 lending data meets a local market with lower prices and faster growth than the UK average. As an illustration, not a lending quote, a 25% deposit on an average Stoke-on-Trent home comes to about £37,800, against about £67,800 on the UK average. Why rent figures differ by publisher is covered in our post on reading the three rental indices.

What should investors take from the summer 2026 lending data?

The summer 2026 lending data says finance was available but nobody was rushing. Fewer landlords competing to buy can mean less competition for the homes that suit a rental. It doesn’t guarantee a bargain, and each property’s numbers still have to work at today’s rates.

Since this was written: approvals for house purchase fell to 53,400 in September 2026 (Bank of England, 29 October 2026 (opens in a new tab)), and Bank Rate rose to 4% on 5 November 2026 (see our Bank Rate post).

Questions investors are asking

What were UK mortgage approvals in June 2026?

Net mortgage approvals for house purchase were 58,200 in June 2026, up from 56,600 in May, according to the Bank of England’s Money and Credit release published on 29 July 2026. That was below the average of around 61,400 over the previous six months. Approvals for remortgaging with a different lender rose to 34,200, from 33,800 in May.

What is the difference between mortgage approvals and mortgage lending?

A mortgage approval is a lender’s agreement to lend for a house purchase, counted before the loan completes, so the Bank of England treats approvals as a sign of future borrowing. Lending is money actually paid out. In June 2026 gross secured lending was £27.4 billion and net mortgage borrowing, after repayments, was £7.7 billion.

Are buy-to-let landlords still buying property?

Fewer landlords were buying than remortgaging in spring 2026. UK Finance figures show 16,026 buy-to-let loans for house purchase in April to June 2026, against 39,631 buy-to-let remortgages, more than two remortgages for every purchase. The average interest cover ratio on new buy-to-let loans was 217%, so the rent on those loans was more than twice the interest.

What was Bank Rate in summer 2026?

Bank Rate was 3.75% through the summer of 2026. The Bank of England’s Monetary Policy Committee held it at 3.75% in the decision published on 30 July 2026, by six votes to three, with three members voting to raise it to 4%. Bank Rate later rose to 4% on 5 November 2026.

Are house prices in Stoke-on-Trent rising?

Yes. HM Land Registry’s first estimate put the average house price in Stoke-on-Trent at £151,355 in May 2026, up 5.0% in a year, against a UK average of £271,295 and growth of 2.7%. Average private rent in Stoke-on-Trent was £707 a month in June 2026, up 4.8%, according to the Office for National Statistics.
