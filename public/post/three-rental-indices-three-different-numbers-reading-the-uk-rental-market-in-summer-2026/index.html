UK Rent Growth Summer 2026: Why ONS, Zoopla and Rightmove Differ

By Valentine Grey8 August 2026Updated 15 November 20267 min read

Three respected organisations measured UK rents in the summer of 2026 and came back with three different numbers. None of them was wrong. Knowing why they differ is one of the more useful skills a property investor can pick up, and the West Midlands shows the gap more clearly than most.

The short answer: the Office for National Statistics put UK rent growth at 3.3% in the year to June 2026, Zoopla at 2.1% for new lets and Rightmove at 2.3% for advertised rents outside London, because each measures a different set of rents. The ONS counts existing and new tenancies; Zoopla and Rightmove track homes coming to market.

Key points

  • The ONS Price Index of Private Rents put the average UK private rent at £1,388 in June 2026, up 3.3% in a year.
  • Zoopla’s June 2026 rental market report put the average UK new-let rent at £1,321, up 2.1% in a year.
  • Rightmove’s Rental Trends Tracker put the average advertised rent outside London at a record £1,397 a month in April to June 2026, up 2.3% in a year.
  • In the West Midlands, the ONS measured 4.4% rent growth in the year to June 2026, while Zoopla’s new-let index showed 0.4%.
  • ONS figures for June 2026 put the average rent in Stafford at £896 (up 6.3%) and in Stoke-on-Trent at £707 (up 4.8%).

Why do the ONS, Zoopla and Rightmove rent figures differ?

UK rent indices differ because they measure different rents: the ONS covers existing and new private tenancies, Zoopla tracks new lets, and Rightmove tracks the rents advertised on new listings.

The Price Index of Private Rents (PIPR) is the Office for National Statistics’ monthly measure of rents on new and existing private tenancies, built from administrative data and classed as official statistics in development. Its release of 22 July 2026 (opens in a new tab) put the average UK rent at £1,388 in June 2026.

Zoopla’s index is “based on asking rents and adjusted to reflect achieved rents”; its June 2026 rental market report (opens in a new tab) gave £1,321, up £30 in a year. Rightmove’s Rental Trends Tracker for April to June 2026 (opens in a new tab) measures advertised rents on new listings, with its headline excluding Greater London.

Timing matters too. In June 2025 the ONS measure was running at 6.7%. It has since slowed towards the lower figures the new-let indices were already showing. That fits how they are built: a new letting reprices at once, while an existing tenancy changes less often, so an index that includes sitting tenants moves later.

Which rent figure should an investor use?

The right rent figure depends on the job. For a property that already has a tenant, the ONS figure is closer to your experience. For a purchase you intend to let, the new-let and asking-rent indices show what the market is offering now, and in summer 2026 they were running below the ONS figure, not above it.

The West Midlands is the clearest example: 4.4% from the ONS, 0.4% from Zoopla, the lowest of any region in its report. Underwrite a purchase on the higher number and you could overestimate the rent. We’d check the local ONS figure for the district, then compare it with what similar homes are achieving today.

Is the UK rental market still short of homes?

The UK rental market was still short of homes in summer 2026. Zoopla’s June 2026 report found 25% fewer rental homes on the market than before the pandemic, with supply 20% to 30% below pre-pandemic levels in every region. Rightmove reported that in April to June 2026 available rental homes fell below the previous year’s level for the first time since 2022.

Demand has cooled from its peak. Zoopla counted 5.6 enquiries per rental home in May 2026, down from 15.5 in 2022. Rightmove counted 10, against 22 at the 2022 peak. Different methods, same direction.

Pay is keeping up. ONS earnings figures for March to May 2026 (opens in a new tab) show regular pay up 3.4% and total pay up 4.3% a year, level with or ahead of rents on all three measures. A tight market, then, not a runaway one. Our post on jobs and pay in Staffordshire covers the local side.

Where were rents rising fastest in summer 2026?

Rents rose fastest in the cheaper parts of the UK in summer 2026. Zoopla found rents in areas averaging under £750 a month rising by nearly 5% a year, while areas above £1,250 grew at or below the 2.1% average. On the ONS measure for the year to June 2026, the North East led the English regions at 6.3% and London was lowest at 2.2%.

What happened to rents in Stoke-on-Trent and Staffordshire?

Rents in most of Staffordshire rose faster than the UK average in the year to June 2026, according to ONS local rent data (opens in a new tab). Stafford’s average rent was £896, up 6.3% from £843, nearly twice the UK rate; Stoke-on-Trent’s was £707, up 4.8%.

On prices, HM Land Registry’s first estimates for May 2026 (opens in a new tab) put Stafford’s average at £263,494 (up 4.7%) and Stoke-on-Trent’s at £151,355 (up 5.0%), against £271,295 and 2.7% for the UK. Later revisions trim those to 4.2% and 4.4%, still ahead of the UK. Lower entry prices with growth above the national average is worth noting, district by district.

What did borrowing cost in summer 2026?

Bank Rate was 3.75% in summer 2026. The Bank of England’s Monetary Policy Committee held it there in the decision published on 30 July 2026 (opens in a new tab), by six votes to three, with three members voting for a rise to 4%. Our post on the June 2026 mortgage data covers what lenders were doing.

Since this was written: ONS figures published on 21 October 2026 put the average UK rent at £1,406 in September 2026 (up 3.8%), Stoke-on-Trent at £718 (up 5.1%) and Stafford at £902 (up 6.5%). Bank Rate rose to 4% on 5 November 2026 (see our Bank Rate post).

Questions investors are asking

What is the Price Index of Private Rents?

The Price Index of Private Rents (PIPR) is the Office for National Statistics’ monthly measure of UK private rents, covering both new and existing tenancies and built from administrative data. It is classed as official statistics in development. In June 2026 it put the average UK private rent at £1,388 a month, up 3.3% in a year.

Why do Zoopla and the ONS show different rent growth?

Zoopla and the ONS measure different rents. Zoopla’s index tracks new lets, while the ONS Price Index of Private Rents covers new and existing tenancies. In summer 2026 Zoopla reported 2.1% growth and the ONS 3.3%. In the West Midlands the gap was wider: 0.4% on Zoopla’s measure against 4.4% from the ONS.

What is the average rent in Stoke-on-Trent?

The average private rent in Stoke-on-Trent was £707 a month in June 2026, up 4.8% on a year earlier, according to the Office for National Statistics. That compares with £971 for the West Midlands region and £1,388 for the UK. Later ONS figures put Stoke-on-Trent at £718 a month in September 2026, up 5.1%.

Are rents rising faster than wages in the UK?

Not on the official figures for summer 2026. The ONS put UK rent growth at 3.3% in the year to June 2026, while its earnings figures for March to May 2026 showed regular pay up 3.4% and total pay up 4.3%. Zoopla’s new-let measure, at 2.1%, was well below pay growth, so affordability for tenants was slowly improving.

Is there still a shortage of rental homes in the UK?

Yes. Zoopla’s June 2026 report found 25% fewer homes to rent than before the pandemic, with supply 20% to 30% below pre-pandemic levels in every region. Rightmove reported that in April to June 2026 the number of available rental homes fell below the previous year’s level for the first time since 2022.

Three numbers, one market. Know which one you’re reading before you price a deal.
