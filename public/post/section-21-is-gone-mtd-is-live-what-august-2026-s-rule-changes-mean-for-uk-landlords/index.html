Section 21 Gone, MTD Live: 2026 Rule Changes for UK Landlords

By Valentine Grey2 August 2026Updated 15 November 20267 min read

Two dates a week apart closed out the summer for landlords. On 31 July 2026, the last chance to start a court claim on an old section 21 notice ran out. On 7 August, the first quarterly update under Making Tax Digital was due at HM Revenue and Customs.

The short answer: by August 2026, section 21 had gone for good in England, and landlords with more than £50,000 of qualifying income were sending quarterly updates to HMRC. One rule is about tenancies and the other about tax, but both reward the same habit: records kept as you go, not pieced together later.

Key points

  • Section 21 “no-fault” evictions ended in England on 1 May 2026, and the last day to start a court claim on a section 21 notice served before then was 31 July 2026.
  • Making Tax Digital for Income Tax has applied since 6 April 2026 to landlords and sole traders whose qualifying income was over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year.
  • The first Making Tax Digital quarterly update, covering 6 April to 5 July 2026, was due on 7 August 2026; the next deadlines are 7 February and 7 May 2027.
  • HMRC will not apply penalty points for late quarterly updates in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, but late tax returns still earn points.
  • The Making Tax Digital threshold falls to £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and £20,000 from 6 April 2028.

What changed for landlords in summer 2026?

In summer 2026, landlords in England lost section 21 and the first group of landlords across the UK moved onto Making Tax Digital. The main 2026 deadlines:

Is section 21 abolished now?

Yes. Section 21 was abolished for private tenancies in England on 1 May 2026 by the Renters’ Rights Act 2025, and the transition window for older notices closed on 31 July 2026. GOV.UK’s guidance on notices given before 1 May 2026 (opens in a new tab) says proceedings had to start by whichever came first, the time left on the notice or 31 July, and that it is “now too late to use it to apply to court if you haven’t done so already.”

A landlord who wants a property back now serves a section 8 notice, which names a legal ground for possession such as rent arrears or a sale. Our guide to what the Renters’ Rights Act changed in May 2026 covers periodic tenancies, rent increases and the other new rules.

Without section 21, a possession claim stands or falls on evidence: a clean rent ledger, dated inspection notes, every notice and how it was served.

What is Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is HM Revenue and Customs’ system that requires landlords and sole traders to keep digital records and send HMRC a summary of their income and expenses every quarter through compatible software. HMRC’s eligibility guidance (opens in a new tab) says anyone with qualifying income over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year should have started using it from 6 April 2026.

Qualifying income is the key term. HMRC’s guidance on working out your qualifying income (opens in a new tab) defines it as your total income from self-employment and property “before expenses (also known as turnover)”. Salary taxed through PAYE, pensions and dividends do not count. If you own a property jointly, only your share of the rent counts.

When are the Making Tax Digital quarterly deadlines?

Making Tax Digital quarterly updates are due on 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May. Each update is cumulative: according to HMRC’s page on sending quarterly updates (opens in a new tab), it covers everything from 6 April to the end of the update period.

The quarterly updates do not replace the tax return. HMRC’s guidance on submitting your tax return (opens in a new tab) says it is due by 31 January following the end of the tax year, which for 2026 to 2027 means 31 January 2028.

What happens if you miss a Making Tax Digital deadline?

In the 2026 to 2027 tax year, a late quarterly update earns no penalty point: HMRC says it “will not apply penalty points for late quarterly updates during the 2026 to 2027 tax year.” Late tax returns still earn points. From 2027 to 2028, each missed deadline earns a late submission point, and reaching 4 points brings a £200 penalty. We’d use this year as a free practice run, not a reason to leave the software unopened.

Will Making Tax Digital apply to me in 2027 or 2028?

Making Tax Digital will apply from 6 April 2027 if your qualifying income was over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026, and from 6 April 2028 if it was over £20,000 in 2026 to 2027. Because it counts rent before expenses, smaller landlords cross the line sooner than they expect.

An illustration, not a forecast. The Office for National Statistics (ONS) Price Index of Private Rents (opens in a new tab), published on 21 October 2026, puts the average rent in Stoke-on-Trent at £718 a month in September 2026, or £8,616 a year. Three homes let at that average bring in £25,848 a year, above the £20,000 threshold, whatever the mortgage and repair bills. Our post on why landlords below the threshold should get ready now sets out the steps.

Are landlords leaving the market after these changes?

Not according to one survey. Handelsbanken’s 2026 Property Investor Report (opens in a new tab), published on 19 May 2026, surveyed 200 UK property investors, managers and landlords in early 2026. 84% said they plan to increase their portfolio holdings, against 54% in the bank’s 2025 survey, and only 1% said they will leave the market within a year.

It is a small sample of professionals, not all landlords, so we’d read it as a mood rather than a measurement. But it fits what the new rules reward: landlords who run property as a business.

What should Staffordshire landlords do now?

  1. Check where your old notices stand. Any section 21 notice not taken to court by 31 July 2026 can no longer be used.
  2. Build an evidence file for each property. Rent ledger, inspections, repairs and every notice served.
  3. Review rent once a year, properly. Use Form 4A with two months’ notice and price it against local evidence.
  4. Work out your qualifying income for 2025 to 2026 and 2026 to 2027, and see which threshold you cross.
  5. Choose software before you have to. If you are already in, the next update is due on 7 February 2027.
  6. Get ready for the landlord database. Stoke-on-Trent and Staffordshire are in the West Midlands, the first region to register, from 15 December 2026. Our Phase 2 guide explains how.

Questions landlords are asking

When is the next Making Tax Digital quarterly deadline?

The next Making Tax Digital quarterly update deadline is 7 February 2027, covering 6 April 2026 to 5 January 2027. The final update for the 2026 to 2027 tax year is due on 7 May 2027, and the tax return itself by 31 January 2028.

Do I need Making Tax Digital if my rent is under £50,000?

Not yet, but the threshold is falling. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies from 6 April 2027 if your qualifying income was over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026, and from 6 April 2028 if it was over £20,000 in 2026 to 2027. Qualifying income is gross rent and self-employment income, before expenses.

Is there a penalty for a late Making Tax Digital quarterly update?

Not in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. HMRC says it will not apply penalty points for late quarterly updates during that year, although late tax returns still earn points. From 2027 to 2028, each missed deadline earns a point, and reaching 4 points brings a £200 penalty.

Does Making Tax Digital replace the Self Assessment tax return?

No. Under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax you send four quarterly updates and still submit a tax return through your software by 31 January following the end of the tax year. For the 2026 to 2027 tax year, that means the return is due by 31 January 2028.
