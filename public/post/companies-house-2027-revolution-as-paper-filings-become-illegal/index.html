Companies House Accounts: Software-Only Filing from April 2028

By GVN Estate Invest30 June 2025Updated 15 November 20266 min read

If your rental property sits in a limited company, its accounts will have to reach Companies House through commercial software from 1 April 2028. That part of the reforms is going ahead. The part many small property companies were bracing for, filing a full profit and loss account, has been paused.

The short answer: from 1 April 2028, Companies House will only accept company accounts filed through commercial software, in iXBRL format, and its web and paper routes close for accounts. On 23 October 2026 the government paused the plan to make small companies and micro-entities file a profit and loss account, so abridged and filleted accounts can carry on.

Key points

  • From 1 April 2028, every UK company must file its accounts with Companies House using commercial software, in iXBRL format. Web and paper filing of accounts closes.
  • Other filings, such as the confirmation statement, can still be made through Companies House’s web service after April 2028.
  • On 23 October 2026 the government paused the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file a profit and loss account. Abridged and filleted accounts remain allowed.
  • Identity verification for company directors became a legal requirement on 18 November 2025, starting a 12-month transition for existing directors.
  • A private company must file its accounts within 9 months of its accounting reference date. Late filing penalties run from £150 to £1,500.

What is changing at Companies House in April 2028?

The main change at Companies House in April 2028 is software-only filing of company accounts. Companies House’s update of 23 October 2026 (opens in a new tab) says all UK registered companies must file accounts in iXBRL format using commercial software from that date, whether they file their own accounts or use an accountant. Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) is a digital format that tags the figures in a set of accounts so a computer can read them.

The powers behind this come from the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 (opens in a new tab). According to Companies House’s Changes to UK company law site (opens in a new tab), these also apply from April 2028:

  • Web and paper routes close for accounts, but stay open for other statutory filings.
  • A stronger audit exemption statement. Any company claiming an audit exemption will need an enhanced statement from its directors on the balance sheet, naming the exemption and confirming the company qualifies.
  • Accounts filed in one go. The component parts of the accounts and reports must be filed together.
  • Limits on shortening the year end. A company will have to give a business reason to shorten its accounting reference period more than once within 5 years.

Do small companies have to file a profit and loss account from 2028?

No. Small companies and micro-entities will not have to file a profit and loss account from April 2028, because the government paused that requirement on 23 October 2026. Companies House says the existing filing requirements continue, and the government will look at joining up Companies House and HMRC filing while the requirement is paused.

Two terms matter here. Abridged accounts, in Companies House’s words, “contain a balance sheet with a sub-set of the information included in a full balance sheet”, and every member of the company has to agree to them. Filleted accounts are accounts filed without the profit and loss account, so those figures stay off the public register. Both stay available.

A special purpose vehicle (SPV), a limited company set up only to hold property, is sized like any other company. Companies House’s accounts guidance (opens in a new tab) sets these limits for accounting periods beginning on or after 6 April 2025. A company must meet at least two of the three:

LimitMicro-entitySmall company
TurnoverUp to £1 millionUp to £15 million
Balance sheet totalUp to £500,000Up to £7.5 million
Average employeesUp to 10Up to 50

The property a company owns counts towards its balance sheet total, so check that line as a portfolio grows.

How has the Companies House timetable changed since 2025?

The Companies House accounts timetable has moved twice since this article was first published in June 2025. The original headline said paper filings would “become illegal” in 2027. That overstated it: the plan was to close the paper and web routes for accounts, and the date has since moved to April 2028.

DateWhat happened
June 2025Reforms planned for April 2027: software-only accounts, profit and loss accounts for small and micro companies, abridged accounts withdrawn.
28 January 2026Companies House said the changes would not be introduced in April 2027, while the reforms were reviewed.
9 June 2026New start date of April 2028 confirmed, giving companies 21 months to prepare. Profit and loss filing, with a publication opt-out, still planned.
23 October 2026Profit and loss requirement paused; abridged and filleted accounts kept; software-only filing confirmed.
1 April 2028Accounts filed on or after this date must come from commercial software.

The 2026 dates come from the change log on GOV.UK’s Filing your Companies House accounts (opens in a new tab) page. Because the plan has changed twice, we’d treat any article on it, this one included, as a starting point and check the Companies House pages before acting.

Do property company directors need to verify their identity?

Yes. Every company director, including the director of a one-property SPV, has to verify their identity with Companies House. The Changes to UK company law site (opens in a new tab) says it became a legal requirement on 18 November 2025, which started a 12-month transition period rather than setting a single deadline.

The GOV.UK verification guidance (opens in a new tab) says an existing director gives their personal code in the company’s next confirmation statement, and the company can’t file that statement unless all its directors are verified. You verify through GOV.UK One Login or through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP), an agent authorised by Companies House to carry out checks. Companies House warns that missing the requirement is an offence that can bring a financial penalty.

What should property company directors do before April 2028?

  1. Ask who files your accounts, and how. If an accountant files through their own software, confirm it produces iXBRL. If you use Companies House’s online service yourself, choose a product from GOV.UK’s list of software for company accounts (opens in a new tab) before your first filing on or after 1 April 2028.
  2. Check your directors are verified. Make sure every director has a personal code before the next confirmation statement is due.
  3. Keep the deadlines. Accounts are due 9 months after the accounting reference date. Companies House charges £150 for up to a month late, rising to £1,500 for more than 6 months.
  4. Think before moving your year end. From April 2028 a second shortening within 5 years needs a business reason.
  5. Revisit structure only with numbers. Our guide to owning personally or through a company covers the trade-offs, and our Budget 2026 landlord tax post explains why the April 2027 rate rise falls on individuals, not companies.

Questions property company directors are asking

Can I still file company accounts on paper?

Yes, until 1 April 2028. Companies House says all accounts filed on or after that date must be filed through commercial software in iXBRL format, and its web and paper routes will close for accounts. Paper and web filing stay open for other statutory filings, such as the confirmation statement.

Will small companies have to file a profit and loss account from 2028?

No. On 23 October 2026 the government paused the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file a profit and loss account with Companies House from April 2028. The existing filing requirements continue, so smaller companies can still leave the profit and loss account off the accounts they file.

Can I still file abridged accounts after April 2028?

Yes. Companies House now says companies can continue to file abridged and filleted accounts from April 2028. The June 2026 plan to withdraw abridged accounts was set aside when the profit and loss requirement was paused on 23 October 2026. The accounts must still be filed through commercial software from 1 April 2028.

Do property company directors have to verify their identity?

Yes. Identity verification became a legal requirement on 18 November 2025, with a 12-month transition for existing directors. A current director gives their personal code in the company’s next confirmation statement, and the company can’t file that statement unless every director is verified. Missing the requirement is an offence.
