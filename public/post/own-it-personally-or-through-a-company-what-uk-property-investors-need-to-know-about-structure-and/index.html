Buy-to-Let in Your Own Name or a Limited Company? UK Tax 2026

By Valentine Grey3 August 2026Updated 15 November 20266 min read

Same house, same street, same rent. Two owners can still pay quite different tax on it, and the difference usually comes down to one decision made before exchange: whose name goes on the title.

The short answer: a limited company pays corporation tax of 19% to 25% and deducts mortgage interest in full, while a landlord who owns personally pays income tax and gets only a basic rate credit on interest. In our illustration, a higher-rate taxpayer keeps more through a company; a basic-rate landlord who draws all the profit keeps more personally.

Key points

  • Landlords who own personally get relief on mortgage interest only as a basic rate tax credit (20% in 2026 to 2027), a restriction fully in force since 6 April 2020.
  • Companies are not affected by that restriction. They pay corporation tax at 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% above £250,000, with marginal relief between.
  • Budget 2026 plans property income tax rates of 24%, 44% and 49% for individuals from 6 April 2027. It is an income tax measure; companies pay corporation tax instead.
  • Profit taken out of a company as dividends is taxed again, at 10.75%, 35.75% or 39.35% above a £500 allowance in 2026 to 2027, with the same rates set for 2027 to 2028.
  • Companies pay the 5% Stamp Duty Land Tax surcharge on residential purchases, and moving property you already own into your company is taxed as a sale at market value.

What is Section 24 and how does it affect landlords who own personally?

Section 24 is the common name for section 24 of the Finance (No. 2) Act 2015 (opens in a new tab), which stopped individual landlords deducting mortgage interest from rental income and gave them a basic rate tax credit instead. Phased in from 2017 to 2018, it is now complete: HMRC’s rental income guidance (opens in a new tab) says “From 6 April 2020 Income Tax relief on all residential property finance costs is restricted to the basic rate of Income Tax.”

A higher-rate taxpayer pays 40% on rental profit but gets 20% back on interest, and that gap is why the company question comes up. From 2027 to 2028, section 6 of the Finance Act 2026 (opens in a new tab) and its Schedule 1 tie the credit to the new “property basic rate”: 22% in the Act as it stands, 24% under the Budget 2026 plans. Our Budget 2026 landlord tax guide covers those rates.

How is a property company taxed?

A property company pays corporation tax on its rental profit after deducting mortgage interest in full: HMRC’s guidance says companies are not affected by the finance cost restriction. A special purpose vehicle (SPV) is a limited company set up only to hold and let property.

The Corporation Tax rates on GOV.UK (opens in a new tab) are 19% (the small profits rate) on profits of £50,000 or less and 25% (the main rate) over £250,000, with marginal relief between; the limits shrink if you have associated companies. Section 11 of the Finance Act 2026 (opens in a new tab) keeps the main rate at 25% for the year from April 2027.

One trap: a close investment-holding company cannot use the 19% rate, under section 18A of the Corporation Tax Act 2010 (opens in a new tab). Companies investing in land let commercially escape that label, but section 18N (opens in a new tab) says a letting is not commercial if the tenant is connected with the company or is a relative of a connected person. A company that lets mainly to your own family can lose the lower rate.

Personal and company ownership differ on almost every tax. Does a company save tax once you take the money out?

A company saves less tax than its 19% rate suggests once you take the profit out, because dividends are taxed again in your hands. An illustration, not a tax calculation: £10,000 of rental profit before £4,000 of mortgage interest, the company on the 19% rate, all profit in one band, the dividend allowance already used, and everything paid out as a dividend.

The higher-rate working: personally, 40% of £10,000 less a 20% credit on the £4,000 of interest is £3,200. In the company, 19% of £6,000 is £1,140, and 35.75% dividend tax on the £4,860 left is £1,737.

The number that matters is what you leave in the company: profit kept there for the next deposit pays only the £1,140 of corporation tax. A company also files accounts and a Company Tax Return (opens in a new tab) every year, a cost the illustration leaves out.

Can you move a property you already own into a limited company?

You can move a property you already own into a limited company, but tax treats it as a sale at market value, whatever price you agree. For Stamp Duty Land Tax, section 53 of the Finance Act 2003 (opens in a new tab) takes the price as not less than market value when the buying company is connected with the seller. For Capital Gains Tax, section 18 of the Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992 (opens in a new tab) treats deals between connected persons as not made at arm’s length.

So both taxes can fall due, and a gain must be reported quickly: see our 60-day Capital Gains Tax guide. Whether any relief applies depends on the facts, so take advice first.

Which structure suits a Staffordshire property investor?

The right structure for a Staffordshire property investor depends on your tax band, whether you need the rent to live on, and how long you will hold. The ONS private rent figures (opens in a new tab) published on 21 October 2026 put average rent in Stoke-on-Trent at £718 a month in September 2026, against £983 across the West Midlands. A company with a handful of lets at those rents stays well inside the £50,000 small profits limit, so 19% is the rate to compare.

  • A company is often worth modelling if you pay higher-rate tax, have a mortgage and will reinvest the profit.
  • Owning personally is often simpler if you pay basic-rate tax, need the income or plan to hold one property long term.

Questions investors are asking

Is it better to buy a buy-to-let through a limited company?

A limited company is often better for a higher-rate taxpayer who will reinvest the profit, because the company deducts mortgage interest in full and pays 19% to 25% corporation tax. A basic-rate taxpayer who needs the income can keep more by owning personally, because dividends are taxed again.

Does the Budget 2026 landlord tax rise apply to limited companies?

No. The Budget 2026 property income rates of 24%, 44% and 49% from April 2027 apply to individuals, partnerships, trustees and personal representatives. A limited company pays corporation tax on its rental profit instead, and the main rate stays at 25% for the year from April 2027, with the small profits rate at 19%.

Can I transfer my rental property into a limited company without paying tax?

Usually not. When a company buys property from someone connected with it, Stamp Duty Land Tax is charged on at least the market value, and for Capital Gains Tax the transfer is treated as not made at arm’s length. Both taxes can be due on the move. Whether any relief applies depends on the facts, so take tax advice first.
