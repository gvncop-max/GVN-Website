Making Tax Digital for Landlords: Who Must Join in 2027 and 2028

By Valentine Grey12 August 2026Updated 15 November 20266 min read

Two average lets in Stoke-on-Trent bring in about £17,200 a year in rent. Three bring in nearly £26,000. That gap, either side of £20,000, decides when HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) will ask you to keep digital records and report your rental income every quarter.

The short answer: Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies from 6 April 2027 to landlords whose qualifying income was over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026, and from 6 April 2028 to those over £20,000 in 2026 to 2027. Landlords over £50,000 joined in April 2026. If you are below the line, you can volunteer now.

Key points

  • Making Tax Digital for Income Tax started on 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025.
  • The threshold falls to £30,000 from 6 April 2027 (based on 2025 to 2026 income) and £20,000 from 6 April 2028 (based on 2026 to 2027 income).
  • Qualifying income is gross: rent and self-employment turnover before expenses, with jointly owned property counted at your share.
  • Landlords in Making Tax Digital send four quarterly updates, due 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May, and a tax return by 31 January.
  • HMRC estimates around 1,077,000 more sole traders and landlords will need to join from April 2027.
  • Property held through a limited company is outside it: on 28 October 2026 the government said it does not intend to introduce Making Tax Digital for Corporation Tax.

What is Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is what HMRC’s guidance, Use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (opens in a new tab) (updated 16 October 2026), calls “HMRC’s new way to do Self Assessment”: you keep digital records in compatible software, send a summary every quarter and file your tax return from the same software.

A quarterly update is a running summary of your income and expenses since 6 April, sent from your software. It is a report, not a tax bill.

When do landlords have to start Making Tax Digital?

Landlords start Making Tax Digital on a date set by their qualifying income in an earlier tax year, according to HMRC’s eligibility guidance (opens in a new tab) (updated 26 March 2026), which covers sole traders and landlords registered for Self Assessment.

£50,0002024 to 20256 April 2026
£30,0002025 to 20266 April 2027
£20,0002026 to 20276 April 2028

The tax year that decides the April 2028 group is the one running now, from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. HMRC will write to you when you need to start, but its guidance (opens in a new tab) adds that “it is still your responsibility to check”. HMRC’s news release of 5 October 2026 (opens in a new tab) estimates around 1,077,000 more sole traders and landlords will join from April 2027.

What counts as qualifying income for Making Tax Digital?

Qualifying income for Making Tax Digital is your total income from self-employment and property before expenses. HMRC’s qualifying income guidance (opens in a new tab) (updated 11 September 2026) calls it “the amount before expenses (also known as turnover)”. So the £20,000 test is about rent received, not profit. Only your share of jointly owned property counts, and PAYE wages, pensions, company dividends and partnership profit shares are left out.

Here is how that looks locally. The ONS private rent figures (opens in a new tab) published on 21 October 2026 put average rent in Stoke-on-Trent at £718 a month in September 2026, or £8,616 a year. Two average lets come to £17,232, under the line. Three come to £25,848, over it. Across the West Midlands the average was £983 a month, so two average lets there (£23,592 a year) already pass £20,000.

What do landlords have to do once they are in Making Tax Digital?

Landlords in Making Tax Digital have three jobs: keep digital records, send quarterly updates and file a tax return by 31 January.

  1. Keep digital records of the amount, date and category of each item, close to the transaction. HMRC’s page on digital records (opens in a new tab) says to keep them “for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline”.
  2. Send quarterly updates for periods ending 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April, due on 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May, under HMRC’s quarterly update guidance (opens in a new tab). Each covers the year so far, so a later update corrects an earlier one.
  3. Submit your tax return by 31 January after the tax year ends, according to HMRC’s tax return guidance (opens in a new tab).

HMRC’s page on choosing software (opens in a new tab) (updated 13 July 2026) notes that “free products are available for those with simple tax affairs”, though they may have limits.

What are the penalties for late Making Tax Digital submissions?

Making Tax Digital uses penalty points: each missed deadline earns one point, and four points for quarterly updates bring a £200 penalty, according to HMRC’s quarterly update guidance (updated 16 October 2026). For the 2026 to 2027 tax year only, “HMRC will not apply penalty points for late quarterly updates”, though a late tax return still earns a point. Volunteers are not penalised for late quarterly updates at all.

Why should landlords below the threshold get ready now?

Landlords below the threshold should get ready now because the year that sets the April 2028 start is already running, and HMRC lets you join early. Its sign-up guidance (opens in a new tab) (updated 13 October 2026) says: “You can sign up for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax before you need to use it to help you get ready.” You must be registered for Self Assessment and have sent a tax return in the last two years.

We’d treat a voluntary year as a rehearsal. Reviewing costs every quarter catches the repair invoice or agent fee a once-a-year shoebox misses, and from April 2027, under the Budget 2026 plans, each £100 of allowable cost saves £24, £44 or £49 of tax. Our Budget 2026 landlord tax guide covers the new rates.

  2. Check it against the table to see which year applies.
  3. Choose software and start recording this quarter, or sign up as a volunteer.

Does Making Tax Digital apply to landlords with a limited company?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax does not apply to property held in a limited company, because a company pays corporation tax, not income tax. The government’s call for evidence on Corporation Tax administration (opens in a new tab), published on 28 October 2026, states that “the government does not intend to introduce Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Corporation Tax.”

That is not a reason on its own to restructure: our guide to owning personally or through a company sets out the wider trade-offs. For the other rule changes of 2026, see our August 2026 round-up.

Questions landlords are asking

Do I need Making Tax Digital if my rent is under £20,000?

Under the rules HMRC has published, a landlord whose qualifying income stays at £20,000 or less does not have to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. Qualifying income is gross rent plus any self-employment turnover, before expenses, so check the full figure and your share of any jointly owned property. You can still sign up early as a volunteer.

Is the Making Tax Digital threshold based on rent or profit?

The Making Tax Digital threshold is based on gross income, not profit. HMRC defines qualifying income as your total income from self-employment and property before expenses, also known as turnover. A landlord with £24,000 of rent and £10,000 of costs in 2026 to 2027 has qualifying income of £24,000, over the £20,000 threshold that applies from April 2028.

Does jointly owned rental property count towards Making Tax Digital?

Yes, but only your share counts towards Making Tax Digital. HMRC’s guidance says your share of the property income counts towards your qualifying income. If you and a partner own a let equally and it brings in £30,000 a year, each of you has £15,000 of qualifying income from it, before adding any other rent or self-employment income.

Do limited companies have to use Making Tax Digital?

No. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords registered for Self Assessment. A limited company pays corporation tax instead, and on 28 October 2026 the government said it does not intend to introduce Making Tax Digital for Corporation Tax. Companies keep filing their Company Tax Return as before.
